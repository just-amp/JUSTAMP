# JUSTAMP · A Tribute

> **If we cannot shape the era,
> then let us witness it, and remember.**

Some things grow old.
That doesn't mean they have to be forgotten.

Thirty years ago, a music player wasn't just a little icon on a phone. It was a window on your desktop. It had buttons, knobs, sliders, and an equalizer that never seemed to stop moving.

We kept our music on noisy hard drives. We left the player sitting right in the middle of the screen. And when we got tired of looking at it, we went searching for another skin.

There were no recommendation engines deciding what we should hear next. There wasn't a feed waiting to be scrolled.

There was just the music, the player, and the people who made things for it.

Someone wrote the code.
Someone drew a skin.
Someone spent a weekend figuring out how to make an effect sound right.
Someone stayed up late and uploaded their latest creation to a forum.

Little things like that became part of the culture.

And somehow, a simple music player became part of people's lives.

## Today, I brought it back to life.

**JUSTAMP**

Not as another modern music app.

As a reminder of what music players used to be.

I didn't want to redesign that experience for 2026. I wanted to preserve it.

No feeds.
No short videos.
No social features competing for your attention.
No recommendation system deciding what comes next.

You choose the music.

The player's job is simply to play it well.

## Sound

Some of the things people used to spend hours tweaking are still worth having.

A classic 10-band equalizer.
18 EQ presets.
The familiar effects and processing that shaped the sound of that era.

BBE. DFX. iZotope Ozone. 3D Cinema Stage.

These aren't here because a specification sheet looks impressive.

They're here because sometimes you hear a certain sound, and suddenly you're back there again.

## 93,603 Skins

**93,603 skins.**

The interesting part isn't the number itself.

It's what the number represents.

Behind every skin was someone who decided to make their player look a little different. Someone who spent an evening moving pixels around, trying colors, changing buttons, and eventually uploading the result for someone else to use.

Those skins appeared on millions of CRT monitors.

They were part of people's desktops, bedrooms, dorm rooms, offices, and late nights.

Most of them haven't been touched in years.

But they're still here.

So JUSTAMP brings those pixels back to life.

Not because the past needs to be made fashionable again.

Just because something made with that much care deserves to be remembered.

## Freedom

A music player should belong to the person using it.

No ads.
No feeds.
No account required just to listen to your own music.

Play it.
Change it.
Tune it.
Reskin it.

You can also step into a separate, paid Hi-Fi workstation

and build a sound system of your own, piece by piece.

Either way, all you need is a pair of headphones and a JUSTAMP.

Make it yours.

That's enough.

## Why build this in 2026?

Because someone has to remember.

Maybe you remember installing your first music player.

Maybe you remember the first pair of headphones that made you realize music could actually sound like that.

Maybe you remember downloading a skin and waiting for it to finish.

Maybe you spent half an hour moving one EQ slider back and forth, trying to find the sound you wanted.

Or maybe you remember sitting at your computer late at night, headphones on, talking to friends while the green spectrum moved with the music.

Those moments are gone.

The software changed.
The hardware changed.
The internet changed.

We can't turn the clock back.

But we can keep a few things from disappearing completely.

That's what JUSTAMP is for.

Not to pretend it's still 1999.

Not to turn the past into a trend.

Just to put something that mattered back into the hands of people who remember it—and people who never got the chance to experience it the first time.

**Don't chase the times.
Remember them.**

**Don't just preserve the past.
Keep it usable.**
